Critical Stakes & Implications

Three fundamental laws that reshape our understanding of listening, attention, and belonging.

01

Law of Linguistic Prioritization

We are predisposed to attend to and process sensory information along linguistically defined lines, often deferring to linguistic representations even when they contradict more accurate direct sensory memories.

Implications:

  • Language acts as a primary filter for reality
  • Linguistic categories override raw sensory data
  • Memory is reconstructed through linguistic frameworks
02

Law of Cognitive Ease/Cost

The efficiency (speed, effortlessness) of the linguistic interface directly influences the likelihood and ease with which certain thoughts or perceptions are engaged, making language a subtle choreographer of attention.

Implications:

  • Grammatical structures facilitate or hinder thought patterns
  • Lexical gaps create cognitive effort
  • Language shapes frequency of cognitive operations
03

Law of Functional Resonance

Beyond semantic understanding, communal belonging can be forged and reality affirmed through shared participation in oral traditions and the recognition of the functional power of sound, irrespective of explicit meaning.

Implications:

  • Sound carries social and cultural work
  • Meaning extends beyond semantic content
  • Oral traditions create community bonds

A New Understanding

This research demands we attend to the "language habits of our community" as the fundamental architecture of our sensory and cognitive world. We must ignore the illusion of universal, objective perception and reframe silence not just as absence of sound, but as linguistic void where categories are lacking.
